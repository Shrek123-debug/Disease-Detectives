# Image suggestions for the Disease Detectives sheet

The sheet is words only (Arial 4.5 pt, 0 margins, two sides). The 2027-style rules allow one 8.5x11 sheet, both sides, so if you give up some text you can paste small pictures. Each picture below is worth roughly 1 inch x 1 inch (about 3-4 lines of text across the page at 4.5 pt). Listed from highest to lowest payoff on real tests.

## Math pictures (best value: they replace the most explanation)
1. **Exposure 2x2 table**: rows Exposed / Unexposed, columns Ill / Well, cells a b c d, with side formulas: attack rate = a/(a+b) and c/(c+d); RR = [a/(a+b)] / [c/(c+d)]; OR = ad/bc; expected = row x column / N; chi-square = sum (O-E)^2/E.
2. **Diagnostic-test 2x2 table**: columns = truly sick / truly healthy, rows = test + / test -, cells TP FP FN TN, with colored brackets showing sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN) and specificity = TN/(TN+FP) (down the COLUMNS) and PPV = TP/(TP+FP) and NPV = TN/(TN+FN) (across the ROWS). The single most tested table.
3. **Case-control 2x2 table** (columns Cases / Controls) with OR = ad/bc and the matched-pair table (b/c discordant pairs).
4. **The "which measure?" flowchart**: cohort or outbreak list -> RR/attack rate; case-control -> OR; cross-sectional -> prevalence; trial -> RR, VE = 1 - RR, ARR, NNT.
5. **Epi-curve shapes (4 small histograms)**: point source (one sharp peak), continuous common source (plateau), intermittent (irregular), propagated (growing waves one incubation apart). Add the exposure-window timeline: first onset - max incubation to last onset - min incubation.
6. **Herd immunity threshold curve**: HIT = 1 - 1/R0 plotted for R0 = 1 to 18 with measles, smallpox, COVID, flu marked; plus the R0 = contacts x probability x duration formula.
7. **SIR curves** (S falling, I peaking, R rising) with the three Euler-step equations.
8. **Normal curve 68-95-99.7** with z = 1.96 and the mean/median/mode positions for left and right skew; box plot with Q1, median, Q3.
9. **Incidence vs prevalence diagram**: horizontal lines for patients over a month with a vertical "point" line and a shaded "period" band (the Yale-style diagram that is asked every year).
10. **Direct age standardization table** (age groups x rate x standard population = expected) and the SMR = observed/expected formula.

## Concept diagrams
11. **Chain of infection** circle (agent, reservoir, portal of exit, mode of transmission, portal of entry, susceptible host) with where to break it.
12. **Epidemiologic triad** triangle (agent, host, environment) next to the person-place-time triad.
13. **Natural history of disease timeline** (susceptibility, subclinical, clinical, recovery/disability/death) with primordial, primary, secondary, tertiary prevention positioned on it and incubation vs latent period marked.
14. **Study design flowcharts**: cohort (exposed/unexposed to disease), case-control (disease/no disease looking back to exposure), RCT (random assignment), cross-sectional snapshot, with the measure each gives.
15. **Hill criteria / Koch / Evans** one-glance lists (Hill's nine with temporality starred).
16. **Outbreak investigation steps** as a numbered ladder (CDC 10-step beside the 13-step list).
17. **Surveillance types grid**: passive / active / sentinel / syndromic with pros, cons and one example each.
18. **Transmission tree**: direct (touch, droplet <1 m, vertical, fecal-oral) vs indirect (airborne <5 micrometres, vehicle, fomite, mechanical vs biological vector).
19. **John Snow 1854 spot map** (Broad Street pump) and the sketch of a spot map vs area map.
20. **Bias map**: selection vs information vs confounding, with the named biases (recall, interviewer, Berkson, lead-time, length-time, Neyman, Hawthorne) in the right box.

## Disease visuals worth a thumbnail
21. **Lyme tick life cycle with white-footed mouse and deer** (dilution effect), **malaria life cycle** (Anopheles, liver, red cells), **cholera**: Snow's pump and ORS.
22. **Foodborne FATTOM wheel** plus the 41-135 F danger-zone thermometer (poultry 165 F, ground meat 160 F).

Printing note: the sheet has 0 margins, but many printers cannot print to the paper edge (typical unprintable strip is about 0.1-0.25 inch). If you will print rather than hand in a digital copy, test-print one page and check that the first and last characters of each line survive.
