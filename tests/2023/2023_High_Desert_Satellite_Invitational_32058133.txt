[STIMULUS] Case Study

In June of 2017, the rural community of Hilldale on the Arizona-Utah border experienced an outbreak of E. coli O157:H7. Most strains cause food poisoning, generally with symptoms of diarrhea and cramping. The O157:H7 strain is worse than "typical" E. coli: in around 5-10% of cases it causes **hemolytic uremic syndrome**, which can cause kidney failure and death. Young children and elderly are particularly susceptible. In this outbreak, 22 people (mostly children) were sickened, four developed hemolytic uremic syndrome, and two died.

[STIMULUS] Case Study

Introduction to Scenario

As a new public health nurse, you are requested to conduct a routine home visit to see Frank, a 76-year-old Hispanic man, who has been your patient for three years. He lives in a 950 sq.ft. one-bedroom, one-bath house in a small, coastal town in Texas (population = 3,000). Since Frank has Medicare benefits, he sees a doctor when he needs to, but not on a regular basis. He says his only complaint is being tired all of the time, which he attributes to not sleeping well at night. Frank adds that he takes frequent naps during the day as he just cannot stay awake. He has a history of poorly managed diabetes with amputation of his left leg six years ago. Frank is 5'9" tall and weighs 297 pounds. He used to smoke two packs of cigarettes per day, but it is difficult to turn off the oxygen. His lifetime pack year history is almost 100 pack years (40 cigarettes per day divided by 20 cigarettes × 50 years). He drinks a six-pack of beer every day, but only after 5:00 p.m. For his family history, his father died from a heart attack at age 67 and his mother died when she was 70. Frank is divorced and has two children who are married—his son lives in Dallas and daughter lives in Houston. He talks to them about once a week; they want him to move to Houston to be close to his daughter, but he doesn't want to leave the small community where he knows everyone. He has COPD and has oxygen delivered via nasal cannula at 2 L/minute. Initial physical examination reveals blood pressure to be 168/92, pulse 102, and respirations 24. His ears, nose, and throat—examination is unremarkable, and lungs reveal decreased breath sounds. Cardiac examination is normal, and he has an obese abdomen without any other gross abnormalities. The patient has 2+ pitting edema on the right lower extremity to mid shin. He has no skin lesions or rash. His stump is pink and blanches easily. He has diminished sensation in the right foot, with complaints of numbness. Pedal pulses are weak. His medical records shows that he has been diagnosed with diabetes mellitus type 2, alcohol-related cirrhosis, chronic bronchitis, and hepatitis C.

30. [frq, 10 pt] What are some factors that influence the role of nurses who work with clients with disabilities?

[STIMULUS] Scenario Continues 1

Clients in rural areas tend to have an overall poorer health status than their urban counterparts, and are more likely to have one or more chronic diseases such as heart disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, hypertension, arthritis and rheumatism, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cancer. Despite the prevalence of chronic health conditions, rural adults are less likely to seek medical care than urban adults, and older rural adults have a lack of services and staff (Bushy & Napolitano, 2018). Rural clients are less likely to practice preventive health behaviors, such as physical activity, using seatbelts, having regular blood pressure checks, having Papanicolaou (Pap) smears, and doing breast self-examinations (Bushy & Napolitano, 2018). They are more likely to smoke, drink alcohol, and be obese (Bushy & Napolitano, 2018). Frank demonstrates signs of many of these. Frank shares that he does not drive. He can easily get from home to the grocery store, post office, bank, or the bar in his wheelchair. If he needs to see the doctor at the VA, he relies on friends from the Foreign War post who drive him. He doesn't like going to the VA because it takes all day to be seen at the clinic in the nearby city. Frank notes that rural living has advantages in that people take care of each other.

33. [frq, 10 pt] What considerations are important for the public health nurse when developing community health programs that are available, accessible, and acceptable to individuals with disabilities?

[STIMULUS] Scenario Continues 3

Research has shown that outcomes improve for clients with disabilities when case management is used (Huber, 2018). The American Nurses Association defines case management as "a collaborative practice model that includes patients, nurses, social workers, physicians, other practitioners, caregivers, and the community. The case management process encompasses communication and facilitates care along a continuum through effective resource coordination. The goals of case management include the achievement of optimal health, access to care, and appropriate utilization of resources, balanced with the patient's right to self-determination." Case management will allow Frank to remain in his home in this rural community if you can offer adequate support.
