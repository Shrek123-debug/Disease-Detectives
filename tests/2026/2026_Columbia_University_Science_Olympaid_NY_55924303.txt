=== Part I: Multiple Choice Questions ===

P1-1. [mcq, 2 pt] Which measure is most appropriate for comparing how widespread a chronic disease is at a single point in time?
    A) Incidence Rate
    B) Prevalence
    C) Case-fatality rate
    D) Relative risk

P1-2. [mcq, 2 pt] Which of the following is NOT one of the thirteen steps for outbreak investigation?
    A) Shut down public places and others to implement control measures
    B) Present findings to the authorities
    C) Test the hypothesis and refine
    D) Immediately declare a national emergency

P1-3. [mcq, 2 pt] During a listeriosis outbreak linked to a food-processing plant, investigators note that the bacteria are isolated from drains and machinery joints. Which corrective action would most effectively prevent recurrence?
    A) Increasing pasteurization temperatures for finished food products
    B) Disinfecting only visible food-contact surfaces daily
    C) Implementing deep-cleaning protocols targeting biofilm-prone areas
    D) Switching the workforce to rotating shifts to reduce contamination

P1-4. [mcq, 2 pt] A county health department launches a program offering annual low-dose CT scans to long-term smokers to detect lung cancer early. Which category of prevention does this represent?
    A) Primary prevention
    B) Secondary prevention
    C) Tertiary prevention
    D) Quaternary prevention

P1-5. [mcq, 2 pt] At a catered event, there were:
    - 120 people who ate chicken; 36 became ill
    - 90 people who did not eat chicken; 9 became ill
    
    What is the risk ratio (attack rate ratio) associated with eating the chicken?
    A) 1.0
    B) 2.0
    C) 3.0
    D) 7.0

P1-6. [mcq, 2 pt] Which of the following statements best distinguishes latent TB infection from active TB disease?
    A) Latent TB requires multi-drug therapy
    B) Latent TB is contagious
    C) Latent TB shows no symptoms and a normal chest X-ray
    D) Active TB always shows a negative tuberculin skin test

P1-7. [mcq, 2 pt] In a community of 40,000 unvaccinated children, an outbreak of wild poliovirus begins. Based on epidemiological data, 1 in 200 infections results in irreversible paralysis. If 10% of the children become infected, how many paralysis cases are expected?
    A) 2
    B) 20
    C) 200
    D) 400

P1-8. [multi, 2 pt] Which of the following statements about Evans’ Postulates are correct? Select all that apply.
    A) It accounts for host susceptibility and genetic factors when determining causation
    B) It considers environmental factors that influence disease occurrence
    C) It requires that the microorganism be isolated from a diseased host and grown in pure culture
    D) It states that all infected individuals must develop clinical disease for the microorganism to be considered causative
    E) It integrates epidemiologic evidence to support a causal relationship between microorganisms and disease.

P1-9. [mcq, 2 pt] A patient presents with fever, rash, and lymphadenopathy. A lab test reveals double-stranded RNA within the pathogen. Which agent is most consistent with this finding?
    A) Mycobacterium tuberculosis
    B) Poliovirus
    C) Ebola virus
    D) Reovirus

The next morning, 50 of the smoothie drinkers reported stomach cramps and bubbling green diarrhea, while only 10 of the Krabby Patty-only eaters got sick. Using this scenario:

P2-14e. [frq, 1 pt] Calculate the relative risk of illness for those who drank the smoothie vs. those who didn’t.
