In mid-January, a metropolitan health department observed a statistically significant increase in emergency department visits for fatigue, nausea, low-grade fever, and transient cognitive slowing. Most cases resolve within 48–72 hours without treatment. However, a small subset of patients, primarily older adults and immunocompromised individuals, develop progressive neurologic symptoms, severe dehydration, and organ inflammation, occasionally requiring ICU admission.

Initial interviews revealed that many affected individuals consumed ready-to-eat packaged "Hearty Winter Soup" sold at grocery stores. Laboratory testing failed to identify a consistent pathogen. Later, heat-resistant protein aggregates in unopened soup containers were detected, along with trace levels of a cleaning solvent used at the manufacturing facility.

Public health officials defined the illness as Soupborne Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome (SMIS). Over the next two months, reported cases declined after a product recall and changes in manufacturing oversight.

=== Part 3: Patterns, Control, and Prevention ===

[STIMULUS] Below is the list of steps taken by local health authorities to control a norovirus outbreak at a camp. Using the word bank, classify each step of the control and prevention process.

**Word Bank:** Isolation | Diagnosis | Disinfection | Immunization | Blocking of Transmission | Health Education | Treatment | Investigation | Notification | Quarantine

P4-6. [matching, 10 pt] Classify each step of the control and prevention process.
    - Campers and staff are taught proper handwashing, food safety, and other hygiene practices to prevent future outbreaks  <->  Blocking of Transmission
    - Local healthcare officials interview camp staff, campers, and the camp doctor as well as conduct tests to determine the source of the outbreak  <->  Diagnosis
    - The camp stops serving the soup, closes off all public areas, and introduces new health standards to ensure the stoppage of the virus  <->  Disinfection
    - The camp doctor tends to the patients and confirms with testing that the sick campers have norovirus  <->  Health Education
    - Campers who were confirmed to have norovirus are kept in separate cabins  <->  Immunization
    - Everyone that came into contact with the sick campers is instructed to stay in their cabin and stay away from healthy campers  <->  Investigation
    - While there is no current vaccine for norovirus, if one existed everyone present at the camp would be vaccinated to prevent any future outbreaks  <->  Isolation
    - The camp doctor informs the campers' parents and local healthcare authorities of the ongoing outbreak  <->  Notification
