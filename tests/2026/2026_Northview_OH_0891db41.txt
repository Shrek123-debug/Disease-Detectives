[STIMULUS] **Questions 66-80 Case Study: Ringworm Outbreak in a Youth Soccer League**

**Background**

In early September, the Maumee Valley Community Health Department received reports of several children in a local youth soccer league developing unexplained skin rashes. The league involved 120 children ages 7–12, divided into eight teams, practicing twice per week on shared athletic fields.

**Initial Observations**

Parents first noticed small, red, itchy patches on their children's skin. These lesions became increasingly circular and scaly over several days. Many families assumed it was eczema, heat rash, or insect bites. However, by the third week, the school nurse at Maumee Valley Elementary had seen similar rashes on multiple students who all played in the same league.

Recognizing the characteristic ring-shaped pattern, she suspected **tinea corporis (ringworm)** and contacted the community health department for guidance.

**Epidemiological Investigation: Case Findings**

A total of **nine confirmed cases** were identified across **three different teams**:

All affected children presented with:
- Circular lesions with raised, scaly borders
- Itching
- Lesions on arms, legs, neck, or upper torso

No systemic symptoms were reported.

- 5 cases on the U10 Wildcats
- 3 cases on the U12 Tornadoes
- 1 case on the U10 Rockets

**Interviews and Observations:** Public health investigators conducted interviews with players, parents, and coaches. They also visited the practice fields and equipment storage areas.

Key findings included:

1. **Shared Equipment**
Many children regularly shared shin guards, goalie gloves, and practice pinnies. These items were **rarely cleaned** between uses.

2. **Hygiene Gaps**
Several players admitted they **did not shower immediately after practice**, instead going home still in their uniforms.

3. **Animal Exposure**
Players from two affected teams frequently interacted with a **stray cat** that lived near Field 3. The cat was observed to have patches of missing fur.

4. **Inconsistent Cleaning Protocols**
Only one of the four practice fields had a routine schedule for cleaning athletic mats used during warm-ups. Other fields had **no regular disinfection procedures**.

**Potential Sources of Transmission**

Based on the findings, investigators identified three major possible transmission pathways:
- **Zoonotic transmission** from the stray cat
- **Fomite transmission** through contaminated shared equipment
- **Skin-to-skin contact**, which is common during soccer play

Given the widespread sharing of equipment across teams and the pattern of cases across multiple age groups, fomite transmission was considered a primary driver, with the stray cat likely serving as an initial reservoir.

**Public Health Concerns**

Although ringworm is not life-threatening, the number of cases had increased steadily over three weeks, suggesting ongoing transmission.

Officials worried about:
- Spread to additional teams
- Introduction into local schools
- Secondary household infections, especially among younger siblings

The health department recommended immediate action to prevent further spread.
