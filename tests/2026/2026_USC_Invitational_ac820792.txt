=== Case Study II: Legionellosis in Los Angeles ===

[STIMULUS] You have now been assigned to your next investigation, after a case of Legionellosis was reported to your department by Keck Hospital of USC.

Legionellosis is an uncommon respiratory infection caused by the bacteria of the genus Legionella. It tends to manifest in two forms, the milder Pontiac fever and the much more severe Legionnaires' disease (LD). LD is a form of acute pneumonia, with symptoms including cough, shortness of breath, fever, muscle pain, nausea, and diarrhea.

It is not spread from person to person. Instead, legionella is naturally found in freshwater environments and grows easily at warm temperatures and still water. It poses a health risk when it is found in water systems and devices, such as air conditioning units, humidifiers, hot tubs, and water fountains, where people can aspirate or inhale mist or spray from contaminated water.

Although isolated cases of Legionellosis are common, out of precaution, you reach out to surrounding hospitals to inquire of other cases with similar symptoms. They get back to you, and you notice there’s a sharp increase in pneumonia cases in the county over the past three weeks.

35. [frq, 4 pt] What is the difference between a cluster and an outbreak? Would you consider these cases an outbreak? Explain why or why not.

[STIMULUS] Sputum samples were collected from the pneumonia patients to the lab for Legionnaires’ testing. Many of them come back positive, confirming cases of Legionnaires' disease.

Similar to other respiratory diseases, there are certain risk factors towards being more susceptible to developing LD over Pontiac Fever. For example, people with preexisting health conditions (such as diabetes, lung disease, immunodeficiency, etc.), former or current smokers, and people who are over the age of 50 are all more likely to contract pneumonia.
