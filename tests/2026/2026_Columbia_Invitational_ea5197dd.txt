=== Part I: Multiple Choice Questions ===

1. [mcq, 2 pt] Which measure is most appropriate for comparing how widespread a chronic disease is at a single point in time?
    A) Incidence Rate
    B) Prevalence
    C) Case-fatality rate
    D) Relative risk

2. [mcq, 2 pt] Which of the following is NOT one of the thirteen steps for outbreak investigation?
    A) Shut down public places and others to implement control measures
    B) Present findings to the authorities
    C) Test the hypothesis and refine
    D) Immediately declare a national emergency

3. [mcq, 2 pt] During a listeriosis outbreak linked to a food-processing plant, investigators note that the bacteria are isolated from drains and machinery joints. Which corrective action would most effectively prevent recurrence?
    A) Increasing pasteurization temperatures for finished food products
    B) Disinfecting only visible food-contact surfaces daily
    C) Implementing deep-cleaning protocols targeting biofilm-prone areas
    D) Switching the workforce to rotating shifts to reduce contamination

4. [mcq, 2 pt] A county health department launches a program offering annual low-dose CT scans to long-term smokers to detect lung cancer early. Which category of prevention does this represent?
    A) Primary prevention
    B) Secondary prevention
    C) Tertiary prevention
    D) Quaternary prevention

5. [mcq, 2 pt] At a catered event, there were:
    - 120 people who ate chicken; 36 became ill
    - 90 people who did not eat chicken; 9 became ill
    
    What is the risk ratio (attack rate ratio) associated with eating the chicken?
    A) 1.0
    B) 2.0
    C) 3.0
    D) 7.0

6. [mcq, 2 pt] Which of the following statements best distinguishes latent TB infection from active TB disease?
    A) Latent TB requires multi-drug therapy
    B) Latent TB is contagious
    C) Latent TB shows no symptoms and a normal chest X-ray
    D) Active TB always shows a negative tuberculin skin test

7. [mcq, 2 pt] In a community of 40,000 unvaccinated children, an outbreak of wild poliovirus begins. Based on epidemiological data, 1 in 200 infections results in irreversible paralysis. If 10% of the children become infected, how many paralysis cases are expected?
    A) 2
    B) 20
    C) 200
    D) 400

8. [multi, 2 pt] Which of the following statements about Evans’ Postulates are correct? Select all that apply.
    A) It accounts for host susceptibility and genetic factors when determining causation
    B) It considers environmental factors that influence disease occurrence
    C) It requires that the microorganism be isolated from a diseased host and grown in pure culture
    D) It states that all infected individuals must develop clinical disease for the microorganism to be considered causative
    E) It integrates epidemiologic evidence to support a causal relationship between microorganisms and disease.

9. [mcq, 2 pt] A patient presents with fever, rash, and lymphadenopathy. A lab test reveals double-stranded RNA within the pathogen. Which agent is most consistent with this finding?
    A) Mycobacterium tuberculosis
    B) Poliovirus
    C) Ebola virus
    D) Reovirus

10. [multi, 5 pt] Select all statements that are **TRUE**.
    A) The clinical approach focuses on the health of individual patients, while the public health approach focuses on the health of populations.
    B) Surveillance is used in public health to identify and monitor health problems, and it typically involves a 5-step process.
    C) Cross-sectional studies follow a group of individuals over time to determine the incidence of disease based on exposure status.
    D) Relative risk compares the probability of disease between exposed and unexposed groups, and it can be accurately calculated even in case-control studies.
    E) Attack rates can be calculated for specific subgroups of a population, such as by age or sex, to identify high-risk groups. Comparing these subgroup-specific rates can help guide targeted interventions.
