=== Case Study #2 ===

[STIMULUS] To evaluate a proposed vaccine for pathogen π, researchers conduct a randomized controlled trial. A total of 12,000 adults between the ages of 18-80 are recruited and then screened to determine if they have any detectable antibodies to this pathogen. This would indicate prior exposure to the pathogen. Participants are then randomly assigned to one of two groups:
- Experimental group: 6,000 individuals receive two injections of the vaccine, administered four weeks apart
- Control group: 6,000 individuals receive two injections of an inert solution on the same schedule

Neither the participants nor the clinicians administering the vaccine or the solution are aware of what each participant is receiving. Subjects are followed for 9 months after the first dose. In order to keep track of the symptoms and their progress with each of the two solutions, participants completed weekly surveys that tracked their symptoms. If pathogen π infection is suspected, medical providers can report clinic visits, hospital admissions, and request diagnostic testing.

Blood samples are collected at the baseline and every two months after to assess antibody production.

Below are the study's findings at the end of the follow-up.

| Outcome | Vaccine Group (n = 6000) | Control Group (n = 6000) |
|---|---|---|
| Detectable antibodies to pathogen π at study end | 5820 | 210 |
| Symptomatic illness consistent with pathogen π | 40 | 160 |
| Laboratory-confirmed pathogen π infection | 18 | 150 |
| Severe pathogen π illness (hospitalization ≥ 3 days) | 3 | 60 |
| Death attributed to pathogen π | 0 | 5 |

III-2F. [frq, 2 pt] Explain why masking both participants and investigators is critical in a study like this. How could lack of blinding distort the results?

III-2G. [frq, 4 pt] Identify up to two potential confounding factors that might affect symptom development or disease severity in this trial. How could researchers reduce the impact of these confounders?
