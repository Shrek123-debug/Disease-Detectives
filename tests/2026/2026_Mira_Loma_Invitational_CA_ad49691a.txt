=== Part 3 – Case Study ===

[STIMULUS] **Aflatoxin exposure and liver cancer risk**

Between 1986–1989, researchers enrolled 18,244 middle-aged men in Shanghai. Each participant completed a dietary questionnaire and provided a single urine sample. The goal: evaluate whether aflatoxin exposure increases risk of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

Aflatoxin is a naturally occurring toxin produced by Aspergillus fungi, which thrives in warm, humid environments. These fungi commonly contaminate staple foods such as corn, peanuts, soybeans, and various grains during growth, harvest, or storage. When ingested, aflatoxin B₁, the most potent form, is metabolized in the liver into reactive toxic intermediates that can bind to DNA and form adducts, particularly the aflatoxin-N7-guanine adduct. These adducts can cause characteristic mutations in the TP53 tumor suppressor gene, impairing the cell's ability to regulate growth and to repair DNA damage. Over time, this mutagenic process contributes to carcinogenesis in the liver, especially in populations with coexisting risk factors such as chronic hepatitis B infection.

By administering dietary questionnaires and measuring urinary aflatoxin metabolites, the researchers aimed to capture a more accurate picture of each participant's true biologic exposure and better understand how this potent environmental carcinogen contributes to hepatocellular carcinoma risk in real-world settings.
