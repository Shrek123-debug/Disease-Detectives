[STIMULUS] The SIR (susceptible – infected – recovered) model can be adopted to describe various diseases, such as Cooties. This model uses some assumptions: the population is well-mixed and its size is constant, rates (such as transmission and removal rates) are constant, and no demography (i.e. births and deaths). The spread of a disease can be modeled by the following SIR equations:

S' = −aSI

I' = aSI − bI

R' = bI

In these equations, S, I, and R denote the number of individuals susceptible, infected, and recovered, respectively at any given time t. Note that t is measured in days and that S, I, R are measured in people. S', I', and R', which denote the rates of change of S, I, and R, respectively, are measured in people per day. Finally, a and b are called the transmission coefficient and the recovery coefficient, respectively.
