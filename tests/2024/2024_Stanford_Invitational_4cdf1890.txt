=== Case Study 1: Norovirus Outbreak (23) ===

[STIMULUS] At the end of 2022, a Norovirus outbreak was determined across multiple states in the US. The CDC and FDA linked the outbreak to oysters harvested in Galveston Bay, Texas, and a recall was placed on all raw oysters harvested at the bay. During the outbreak, the CDC collected 211 reports of Norovirus from 8 different states.

CS1-1. [frq, 2 pt] Are health providers and local departments required to report cases of the Norovirus?

CS1-2. [frq, 4 pt] List two diseases that health providers and local departments are required to report.

CS1-3. [frq, 2 pt] Why are Norovirus outbreaks more likely to occur from November through April in the northern hemisphere?

CS1-4. [frq, 2 pt] In response to the outbreak, the oysters were recalled. Explain what recalling is and what kind of prevention recalling is.

CS1-5. [frq, 3 pt] What other actions should local restaurants and supermarkets do to prevent the outbreak from happening? List two actions.

CS1-6. [frq, 2 pt] What are some symptoms of Norovirus? List two.

CS1-7a. [frq, 1.5 pt] A professor at Standford eats raw oysters on December 1st, 2022 as a 6 am snack and gets the Norovirus. When would the professor first start to feel symptoms?

CS1-7b. [frq, 1.5 pt] A professor at Standford eats raw oysters on December 1st, 2022 as a 6 am snack and gets the Norovirus. When would the professor be well enough to return to teaching?

CS1-8. [frq, 2 pt] Your friend gets their hands on a batch of raw oysters that was sold during the recall time period. Your friend thinks that it will be okay if they cook the oysters, as that will kill the virus. Based on your knowledge, will cooking destroy the virus? If it does, is cooking and then consuming the oysters still a good idea?

CS1-9. [frq, 2 pt] List two ways people can protect themselves against Norovirus.


=== Case Study 2: UC Burkeley Food Poisoning Outbreak (37) ===

[STIMULUS] In December 2023, UC Burkeley students were suffering from final exams. However, at the same time, an even more sinister malady was afoot. Burkeley students can eat at any of the four dining halls – Cafe 3.14, Feethill, Crosspaths, and Clock Kerr – all of which serve different foods from different sources/providers. Let us assume that all students tend to eat at the dining hall closest to their dorm, but will occasionally eat at a different one. Students also have the option of having their friends/roommates pick up food for them, and many sick students opt for pick up instead of going to eat in the dining halls themselves.

A few days before finals start, students begin complaining about stomach pains, diarrhea, and vomiting. Visiting students from Standford, concerned about their fellow UC Burkeley students, suspect that there is a food poisoning outbreak and collect the following data on students over a period of a week (7 days):

| Dining Hall | Number of sick students | Number of students that eat the dining hall regularly |

| Cafe 3.14 | 250 | 2,000 |
| Feethill | 20 | 3,000 |
| Crosspaths | 45 | 4,000 |
| Clock Kerr | 5 | 1,000 |
| Total | 320 | 10,000 |

=== Case Study 3: Vaccines for babies (23) ===

[STIMULUS] In the city of Standford, a mysterious disease has been plaguing the citizens. The disease seems to particularly affect babies and infants, and while older children and adults can catch the disease, they often remain asymptomatic. Researchers scramble to develop screening tests, since they have (somehow) discovered that when babies and infants get treatment during the early phases of infection, the babies and infants are more likely to recover quickly and with less complications. However, while the screening test that the researchers have developed is rather cheap, the treatment is very expensive. There is also an alternative laboratory test that is extremely accurate and precise that the researchers can perform, however that test is very expensive.

Researchers take a random sample of newborn infants and babies and have them use the screening test and run lab tests on them to see if they are truly sick. They collect the data below:

|  | Is Sick | Is not sick | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive Test | 185 | 20 | 205 |
| Negative Test | 15 | 230 | 245 |
| Total | 200 | 250 | 550 |
