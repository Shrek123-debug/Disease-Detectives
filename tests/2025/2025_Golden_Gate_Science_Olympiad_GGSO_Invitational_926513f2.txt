=== Case Study 1: Citrus Crisis ===

[STIMULUS] Eric and Aidan, citrus aficionados, drop out of Berkeley to start their own orange farm. But soon they notice a problem with some of their plants…. === Case Study 2: West Nile Virus ===

[STIMULUS] Lately, Audrey has been noticing that many of her friends, various students at Stanford and UC Berkeley, have been contracting West Nile Virus. Due to Audrey's interest in epidemiology, she wishes to learn more about the potential outbreak from a public health perspective. She turns to ChatGPT to assist her in her studies.

Audrey gave details about the situation to ChatGPT. Here is the report it created:

"An outbreak of West Nile Virus (WNV) has been identified at Stanford University and the University of California, Berkeley, raising concerns within both academic and public health communities. As of December 2024, a total of 23 confirmed cases have been reported across both campuses, with 14 cases at Stanford and 9 cases at UC Berkeley. Among these, three individuals have developed severe neuroinvasive disease, necessitating hospitalization. No fatalities have been reported to date, though two patients remain in critical condition.

The outbreak was first detected in early November when a cluster of febrile illnesses was identified among Stanford students living in on-campus housing near wetlands. Subsequent testing confirmed WNV as the causative agent. By mid-November, similar symptoms began appearing at UC Berkeley, with cases primarily clustered among students residing near the Strawberry Creek area. Environmental testing has confirmed the presence of WNV in local mosquito populations, with high infection rates in Culex species, the primary vectors of the virus."

Both universities have implemented control measures in collaboration with local health departments. These include targeted mosquito control efforts, such as larvicide applications and increased surveillance of mosquito populations. Educational campaigns are underway to inform students and staff about personal protective measures, such as using insect repellent, wearing long-sleeved clothing, and minimizing outdoor activities during peak mosquito activity at dawn and dusk.

[STIMULUS] Researchers have proposed controlling ACP populations through RNAi. To answer the following questions, first read the following excerpts from Taning, et al. (2016). You aren't expected to have any prior knowledge of RNAi.

"RNAi, the process in which double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) exerts a silencing effect on complementary mRNA, has become a useful research tool in entomology for functional genomics. Characteristics such as easy applications, highly specific targeting, and lack of environmental persistence, make RNAi approaches desirable for crop protection against many insect pests. The RNAi event comprises two major steps: first, the dsRNA should be taken up by cells and then subsequently processed by the cellular core RNAi machinery, thus triggering the silencing of the target gene. These two steps are regulated by many key genes which play central roles in determining the efficacy of RNAi and can result in significant differences in RNAi efficiency between different insect species."

"Five ACP-specific dsRNAs were designed to target genes associated with different biological processes, namely: (1) Energy mobilization: Arginine Kinase (AK…) (2) Cell metabolism: pterin-4-alpha-carbinolamine dehydratase (PCDB1…); (3) Antioxidant defense: Superoxide Dismutase (SOD…); (4) Nervous system: Tomosyn, (TOM…); (5) Reproduction: Vitellogenin (VIT…)."

"The dsRNAs were evaluated regarding efficacy to induce RNAi in ACP using the iPS feeding bioassay. ACP were given feeding access for 15 days on citrus flush treated separately with each of the five designed dsRNAs, with a non-ACP dsRNA (dsGFP), and with no dsRNA (water control). ACP overall mortality scored at the end of the 15 days period varied depending on dsRNA ingested (Fig. 4A). Statistically significant mortality was induced only in insects which fed on flushes treated with dsAK (52%) and dsSOD (46%). Non-significant, lower mortality was observed in insects which fed on flushes treated with dsPCDB1 (8%), dsTOM (15%) and dsVIT (16%), which were not statistically different from the control dsGFP (4%) and water (5%) (Fig. 4A). In general, insect mortality started at 6 dpf, increasing overtime reaching a peak at 13 dpf."
