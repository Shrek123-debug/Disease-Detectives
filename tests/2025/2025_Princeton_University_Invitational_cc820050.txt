[STIMULUS] **Case Study #2:**

In the mid-1980s, pancreatic cancer was among the top ten causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Approximately 35,000 new cases and 30,000 attributable deaths occurred annually in the United States alone. Pancreatic cancer's poor prognosis made identifying modifiable risk factors a public health priority. One area of interest was coffee consumption, a common habit among adults worldwide.

To investigate this, researchers conducted a study starting in 1985. A total of 50,000 adults aged 30–79 years were enrolled and followed for 10 years to evaluate the relationship between coffee consumption and the incidence of pancreatic cancer. Baseline data on coffee consumption, smoking status, age, sex, and other dietary habits were collected using structured questionnaires. Participants were divided into two groups: coffee drinkers (≥1 cup/day) and non-coffee drinkers.

The participants were followed annually, and any new cases of pancreatic cancer were confirmed through medical records and cancer registries. By the end of the study, 200 cases of pancreatic cancer were identified.
