=== Section B: Calculation Questions ===

B1. [numeric, 4 pt] A city of 100,000 people experiences 1,000 deaths from various causes over a one-year period. What is the mortality rate in the city per 1,000 population?

B2. [numeric, 4 pt] A cohort study follows 200 individuals for 5 years. During the study period, 5 people develop a disease, and each person contributes 5 years of observation. What is the person-time rate of disease in this cohort per 1,000 person-years?

B3. [numeric, 4 pt] A study shows that the risk of lung cancer among smokers is 0.15, while the risk among non-smokers is 0.03. What is the population attributable risk of smoking for lung cancer in the population, assuming 40% of the population smokes?

B4. [numeric, 4 pt] A group of 1,000 individuals are exposed to a risk factor, and 50 develop a disease. In a non-exposed group of 1,000 individuals, 20 develop the same disease. What is the risk difference (attributable risk) between the exposed and non-exposed groups?

B5. [frq, 4 pt] In a case-control study of a new drug, 200 people with the disease (cases) and 400 healthy individuals (controls) were asked about their exposure to the drug. Among the cases, 120 had used the drug, while 80 had not. Among the controls, 100 had used the drug, and 300 had not. What is the odds ratio for the association between the drug and the disease?

B6. [frq, 4 pt] A study reports that among smokers, the incidence of lung cancer is 50 cases per 100,000 person-years. Among non-smokers, the incidence is 10 cases per 100,000 person-years. What is the relative risk of lung cancer for smokers compared to non-smokers?

B7. [frq, 4 pt] In a small community of 1,200 people, 100 individuals become ill with food poisoning after a banquet. What is the attack rate for the food poisoning in this population?

B8. [frq, 4 pt] A town has a population of 50,000 people. During the year, 200 new cases of influenza are reported. What is the incidence rate of influenza in the population per 1,000 people?

B9. [frq, 4 pt] An outbreak of a respiratory disease affects 500 individuals, and 30 people die from the disease. What is the case fatality rate of the disease?

B10. [frq, 4 pt] In a city of 250,000 people, 10,000 individuals are known to be living with diabetes. What is the prevalence rate of diabetes in this population per 1,000 people?

B11. [frq, 4 pt] A cohort study follows 500 individuals for 10 years. Over the course of the study, 40 individuals develop heart disease. What is the cumulative incidence of heart disease in this cohort?

B12. [frq, 4 pt] The basic reproductive number (R₀) for an infectious disease is 2. If a vaccination campaign successfully vaccinates 75% of the population, what is the herd immunity threshold (the percentage of the population that needs to be immune to prevent the spread of the disease)?

B13. [frq, 4 pt] A population of 1,000 people has an expected number of 50 deaths based on age-specific death rates from a standard population. However, 60 deaths actually occurred. What is the standardized mortality ratio (SMR)?

B14. [frq, 4 pt] In a study, the risk of a disease is found to be 0.2 with a standard error of 0.05. Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the risk.

B15. [frq, 4 pt] In a population of 100,000 people, 50 people die of a disease before the age of 65. If the average age at death for these individuals is 40, how many years of potential life lost (YPLL) occurred due to this disease?

Note: Individuals also underwent the gold-standard test for NF infection.

| Test Result | **NF Present** | **NF Absent** | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | 45 | 47 | 92 |
| **Negative** | 5 | 903 | 908 |
| Total | 50 | 950 | 1,000 |

C2. [frq, 5 pt] Discuss how the prevalence of NF in the study population impacts the aforementioned statistics.

| Outcome | **Vaccinated** | **Placebo** | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Infected** | 30 | 150 | 180 |
| **Not infected** | 470 | 350 | 820 |
| Total | 500 | 500 | 1,000 |
