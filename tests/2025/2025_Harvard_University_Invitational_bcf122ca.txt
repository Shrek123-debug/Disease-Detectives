Blood samples are collected from all participants at monthly intervals and tested for antibody to agent X. The results are shown below:

[mcq, 2 pt] In Pulse-Field Gel Electrophoresis (PFGE), a key technique used for DNA fingerprinting, the separation of large DNA fragments is achieved through the application of alternating electric fields. Which of the following factors most significantly affects the resolution of large DNA fragments during PFGE, and how is this factor typically optimized?
    A) Agarose concentration: Increasing the agarose concentration improves the resolution by providing a denser gel matrix, which can more effectively separate large DNA fragments by size.
    B) Voltage gradient: A higher voltage gradient improves the resolution of large DNA fragments by creating a stronger electric field, which causes faster migration through the gel.
    C) Pulse time and frequency: Optimizing pulse times and frequencies allows for better resolution of large DNA fragments by preventing the molecules from overshooting their positions during migration, improving separation based on size.
    D) Buffer composition: The buffer composition controls the ionic strength and pH, but its effect on resolution is minimal compared to factors like voltage and pulse time during PFGE.
