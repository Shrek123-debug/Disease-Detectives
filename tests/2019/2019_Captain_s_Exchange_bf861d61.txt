[STIMULUS] Use the information below and your knowledge of food-borne illness to answer questions 30-32.

In the beginning of 2017, 4 outbreaks of Salmonella were registered in the United States. Altogether 251 people were infected and 2 died. The source was identified to be contaminated Maradol papayas imported from 4 farms in different regions of Mexico, which is the largest exporter of fresh papayas to the US (65% of imports). In 2011, the FDA (Food and Drug Administration) introduced Import Alert 21-17, according to which papaya shipments from Mexico are tested for Salmonella at the border, and pass into the country only if they test negative. Positive shipments are detained, but if a firm exporting to the US tests negative in 5 consecutive shipments, it can request to be moved to the ''Green List'' of IA 21-17. This allows the shipment to enter the US without being tested.

[STIMULUS] Campylobacter and Salmonella are leading causes of bacterial gastroenteritis in the United States and are estimated to cause more than 1 million episodes of domestically acquired illness annually. Campylobacter and Salmonella are primarily transmitted through contaminated food, but animal-to-human and human-to-human transmission can also occur. Most people who become ill with campylobacteriosis get diarrhea, cramping, abdominal pain, and fever within two to five days after exposure to the organism. In people with compromised immune systems, Campylobacter occasionally spreads to the bloodstream and causes a serious life-threatening condition.

Approximately 76 people with Campylobacter infections die each year. The most common species of the genus is Campylobacter jejuni, which are spiral-shaped gram-negative bacteria which grow optimally at 37 to 42 °C. This range of incubation temperatures is ideally suited for growth in birds, which have a body temperature of approximately 41 to 42 °C. Most people with salmonellosis develop diarrhea, fever, and abdominal cramps between 12 and 72 hours after infection. Salmonellosis can be fatal when infection spreads from the intestines to the bloodstream. Approximately 450 people die each year from salmonellosis in the United States. Salmonella are gram-negative, rod-shaped bacteria that can be divided into two species: S. enterica and S. bongori. There are more than 30 different serotypes of Salmonella; the most common serotypes are Salmonella Typherimurium, Enteriditis, Newport, and Javiana.
