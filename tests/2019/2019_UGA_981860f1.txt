[STIMULUS] This question is based on a true story. In 1846 at Vienna General Hospital, women were dying at extremely high mortality rates shortly after giving birth from puerperal fever. New doctor Ignaz Semmelweis set out to investigate. He compared two maternity wards in the hospital- one which was staffed by doctors and medical students who worked in other parts of the hospital, including the morgue, the pediatric unit, the surgery unit, and more, and one staffed solely by midwives working only in that ward. By recording data, he realized the death rate in the ward staffed by doctors and medical students was five times higher. Doctors had also fallen ill to the puerperal fever; not only pregnant women were susceptible.
