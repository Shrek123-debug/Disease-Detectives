=== Part A: A Salmonella Outbreak in Southern California ===

[STIMULUS] A Salmonella serotype Enteritidis epidemic emerged in the 1980s, when increasing numbers of infections were detected in the Northeastern and Mid-Atlantic regions of the United States. In the early 1990s, while SE rates in the Northeast began to decline, the SE epidemic expanded to the Pacific region. Nationwide, the number of SE isolates reported to CDC peaked at 3.8 per 100,000 population in 1995. Although rates of culture-confirmed SE infection reported to CDC declined to 1.9 by 1999, rates did not decline further through 2001, and outbreaks continue to occur.

In March of 2002, outbreaks of gastroenteritis occurred among schools throughout Southern California. The California Institute of Technology became worried that these outbreaks would jeopardize the Southern California State Science Olympiad Tournament. Representatives from Caltech travelled to Fullerton, CA where one of the best teams of Disease Detectives resided. In this exercise, you will follow the steps of these legendary Disease Detectives as they investigate this Salmonella outbreak.

In the cases of Salmonella studied by Caltech, patients were characterized by abdominal pains, fever, diarrhea, and chills. If left untreated – especially in younger children – Salmonella could easily be fatal. The key to minimizing damages caused by Salmonella was getting proper expedient treatment and care.

=== Part B: Outbreak at MIT ===

[STIMULUS] After the 2016 MIT Science Olympiad Invitational, dozens of teams gathered and decided to have a potluck. As the smells of regional foods mingled in the chilly night air, a light snow embraced the Science Olympiad teams. All was well.

However, tragedy soon struck. Several students reported vomiting and nausea the next day. Reports began flying in from nearby hospitals of students with severe gastrointestinal infections. Of course, the first place Disease Detectives team at the MIT Invitational was invited to take a look at what had just gone down.
