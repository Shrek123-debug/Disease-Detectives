The source was identified to be contaminated Maradol papayas imported from 4 farms in different regions of Mexico, which is the largest exporter of fresh papayas to the US (65% of imports). **Minamata disease: ''the dancing cat fever''**
[Information adapted from Boston University Sustainability: Minamata Disease and Harada, M., 1995]

In 1956, authorities in the fishing city of Minamata, Japan were alerted by an 'epidemic' of unusual behaviors and symptoms displayed by feral cats and patients admitted at the local hospital. Cats were staggering and tilting, while people would have difficulty moving, weak muscles and, in extreme cases, would fall into a coma. The cause was later recognized to be poisoning with methylmercury, a toxic byproduct released into the bay since 1932 by the local factory.
