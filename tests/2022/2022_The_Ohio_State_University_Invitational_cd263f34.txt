=== Part IV- Food Poisoning ===

[STIMULUS] Use the following outbreak to answer the next five questions

At a recent tailgate party in Columbus, Ohio at least 50 buckeye fans became ill with what appeared to be food poisoning. Three clinics in the area treated and released 38 attendees who complained of nausea and vomiting; others with similar symptoms were kept overnight. A health official noted that all of the patients had eaten a pregame lunch at the party host's house.

The tailgate lunch menu included grilled chicken, hot dogs, and a vegetable lasagna dish. Because some of the people who became ill were vegetarian, there was particular interest in determining whether the lasagna might be contaminated. At the time of the report, health officials were still investigating the source of the outbreak. The party host was cooperating in the investigation and had refrained from further serving any food.

=== Part V- Cancer ===

[STIMULUS] 

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the second most common cause of cancer mortality in the United States. Recent studies have found that appropriate screening can reduce CRC mortality. Modifiable risk factors, such as smoking and high alcohol consumption, are also associated with both CRC incidence and deaths.

The investigators undertook a study design to identify if other behaviors or risk factors, apart from genetic mutation, may be risk factors of CRC. All 200 participants who developed CRC and 400 randomly selected participants without CRC from the Precision Medicine Study were included in this study. All consented participants completed a computer-based questionnaire during the follow-up visits, including questions about smoking history, alcohol consumption history, daily exercise, history of colonoscopy screening, as well as socioeconomic characteristics.

Among participants who had CRC, 142 had a smoking history, 108 had more than 10 drinks per week, and 37 had a colonoscopy screening in the past 10 years. Among participants who did not have CRC, 137 had a smoking history, 197 had more than 10 drinks per week, and 199 had a colonoscopy screening in the past 10 years.
