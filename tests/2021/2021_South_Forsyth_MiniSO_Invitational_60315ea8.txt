=== Natural History of Disease ===

[STIMULUS] In January, Disease Detectives Coach Mr. Stevens started hearing about a new disease called Covid-19. By late February concerns were growing about the disease and there were discussions about not going to the regional Science Olympiad competition, however the decision was made to attend, and Mr. Stevens was able to proctor the Disease Detectives event.

Shortly after returning from the competition, all teams were informed that one of the competitors has tested positive. The student testing positive competed in Boomilever, Anatomy, Disease Detectives and Dynamic Planet.

On March 12th, Mr. Stevens developed a fever and noticed a loss of smell. It was mild at first so he wasn’t worried, but by the 14th his symptoms had gotten worse so he phoned his doctor. His doctor recommended he be tested for Covid-19. The test was performed by driving through the hospital parking lot and being tested from his car. The test came back positive later that day. Mr. Stevens was told to quarantine at home and inform his doctor if the symptoms worsened. On March 19th, his breathing became difficult and he was admitted to the hospital. Two days later on the 21st he was unable to breathe on his own and was placed on a respirator in the ICU. After 6 days, on the 27th, he was once again able to breathe on his own and was taken off the respirator. On the 29th, he was moved out of the ICU into a regular hospital bed where he continued to improve. On April 4th, he was released from the hospital and returned home to recover.

=== Case Study 1 ===

[STIMULUS] Approximately 300 persons attended a retirement party at a Silicon Valley internet company held on May 27, 2019. Most of the attendees worked at the Capitol. A private caterer (Caterer A) prepared and served food for the reception. Based on initial telephone interviews of persons reporting illness, the predominant symptoms were nausea and diarrhea, and the incubation period was approximately 24-30 hours.

The following foods were served at the retirement reception: Swedish meatballs, taco dip, crab dip, a vegetable tray and herbed ranch dip, cake, nuts and mints. The vegetable tray consisted of cucumbers, broccoli, cauliflower, carrots, celery, green peppers, and radishes. All foods were prepared onsite on the day of the reception with the exception of the nuts, which were purchased by a coworker, and the mints, which were made by a coworker.

The Swedish meatballs consisted of ground beef, ground pork, sour cream and flour. The meatballs were cooked twice. The taco dip contained layers of cream cheese mixed with salsa, ground beef, tomatoes, lettuce, onion, cheese and salsa. The taco dip was prepared manually by Caterer A in the kitchen at the company, and was not cooked after assembly. The crab dip contained canned real crab, cream cheese and ketchup.

The investigators received completed surveys from 212 attendees. Of those 212 attendees, 127 persons reported a gastrointestinal illness within 72 hours of the reception. The average interval between time of food consumption and onset of illness was 32.3 hours (range 6 to 67 hours).

Persons working in the security office at the company ate samples of the items served at the reception except the Taco Dip. None of the people from this office reported illness. Table 1 shows the association of illness and eating the taco dip.

Table 1: Association of Illness and Exposure to Taco Dip

| Yes | 112 | 8 |
| No | 15 | 77 |
