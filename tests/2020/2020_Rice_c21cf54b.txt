=== Scenario #1 ===

[STIMULUS] In September, an outbreak of Ebola occurred in North Kivu, DRC. Out of a population of 5,000 otherwise healthy individuals, 156 got sick. Later, it was revealed that 127 of those who had gotten sick had recently consumed bush meat. Epidemiologists then randomly chose 50 people from the population who had not gotten sick. They found that 18% of these had consumed bush meat.

P2-1. [frq, 1 pt] Which type of study is being conducted? List 2 pros and 2 cons for this type of study.

P2-3. [frq, 1 pt] What quantity should be used to determine the relationship between having eating bush meat and actually getting infected? Calculate and interpret this quantity.

P2-4. [frq, 1 pt] What are three ways to prevent further ebola cases?


=== Scenario #2 ===

[STIMULUS] Dr. Schwarz Eigenwitz is testing a new method for HIV testing which is significantly cheaper and easier to produce than standard diagnostic tools. He decides to test his product in the population of Town, USA. He collects the following data:

Town population: 10,000 people
True positives: 110 people
True negatives: 9000
False positives: 870
False negatives: 20

P3-1a. [frq, 1 pt] Calculate Sensitivity

P3-1b. [frq, 1 pt] Calculate Specificity

P3-1c. [frq, 1 pt] Calculate Predictive Value Positive

P3-1d. [frq, 1 pt] Calculate Predictive Value Negative

P3-2. [frq, 1 pt] If Dr. Eigenwitz were to take his test to Sub Saharan Africa, with a much greater prevalence of HIV, which of the above calculated quantity/quantities could be expected to increase? Explain briefly why this is true.

P3-3. [frq, 1 pt] In Sub Saharan Africa, which quantity/quantities would remain the same? Explain briefly why this is true.

P3-4. [frq, 1 pt] Dr. Eigenwitz decides that anyone who tests positive for HIV should go through another round of testing. The results of this second round will then be accepted. Would overall specificity decrease or increase or remain the same? Explain briefly why this is true.
