[STIMULUS] 178 people were in a restaurant on January 14th. 42% of those customers had the house salad. The rest did not. 72% of the customers who had the house salad experienced some sort of stomach sickness after going to the restaurant. 12% of the customers who did not have a house salad also experienced some sort of stomach sickness after going to the restaurant. Epidemiologists were notified of this happening. They decided to look into how the house salad exposure affected stomach illness for those restaurant customers.

9a. [frq, 5 pt] What kind of study did the epidemiologists perform? List two advantages and disadvantages for this study?

9c. [frq, 3 pt] Calculate the relative risk using your 2x2 table.

9d. [frq, 3 pt] Calculate the odds ratio using your 2x2 table.

9e. [frq, 4 pt] Calculate attack rates for both exposed and unexposed to the house salad.

9f. [frq, 2 pt] Would odds ratio or relative risk be more relevant to compute for the type of study you determined in part (a)? Why?

[STIMULUS] A group of 1000 people live in a certain neighborhood of Large City. Large City has been known for its air pollution for quite some time. 23% of residents in this neighborhood contracted tuberculosis in two months. Epidemiologists researched this specific neighborhood for two months, due to the large amount of residents contracting tuberculosis.

10a. [frq, 3 pt] Sandy, an epidemiologist, stated that air pollution is the sole cause of increase of TB was a water fountain in a park. She only interviewed people who drank water out of that fountain. What is the flaw to her logic? What do we call this bias? How should she better sample people?

10b. [frq, 2 pt] Air pollution of the area was looked into. Jeremy attempted to measure the air pollution levels. His reading was only 30% accurate. What kind of bias is this, and how can it be prevented?

10c. [frq, 4 pt] Name two ways one could reduce random error. For each suggestion, suggest why it may not be feasible to have improvements.
