=== Part I: Background and Surveillance ===

1. [mcq, 1 pt] The traditional epidemiological triad models holds that infectious diseases result from interactions between which of the following?
    A) Reservoir, portal of entry, host
    B) Agent, host, environment
    C) Agent, host, reservoir
    D) Host, environment, reservoir

2. [mcq, 1 pt] Which of the following statement is true regarding human reservoirs?
    A) Very few infectious diseases have human reservoirs
    B) Human reservoirs require an intermediary, such as a tick, to transmit infection
    C) Human reservoirs frequently transmit infections to animals
    D) Human reservoirs may or may not show effects of illness

3. [mcq, 1 pt] What must an epidemiologist do before determine the number of cases of disease in a given population?
    A) Identify factors that may be contributing to disease
    B) Report results and recommend public health action
    C) Perform descriptive epidemiology according to time, place, and person
    D) Determine a set of standard criteria for classifying a case of disease

    A) The desired level of disease in a community, usually zero.
    B) The amount of a particular disease that is usually present in a community.
    C) An aggregation of cases of disease grouped in a place and time.
    D) An increase in the number of cases of a disease above what is normally expected in that population in that area.

5. [mcq, 1 pt] Which of the following is NOT an example of an indirect mode of transmission?
    A) Droplet spread
    B) Vehicle borne
    C) Airborne
    D) Vector borne

6. [mcq, 1 pt] Which of the following is most likely to result in an epidemic?
    A) A decrease in the virulence of the agent
    B) The recent introduction of the agent into a setting where it has not been before
    C) A decrease in host exposure
    D) A decrease in the susceptibility of the host

    A) Passive surveillance

=== Part II: Outbreak Investigation ===

[STIMULUS] Directions: Use the following case study to answer questions 16-18.

The Department of Health received a telephone call from a university student stating that she and her roommate were suffering from nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Both had become ill overnight. She had taken an over-the-counter medication that had given some relief to her symptoms. Neither she nor her roommate had seen a doctor or gone to the emergency room. The students believed their illness was due to the salads they had eaten at a local restaurant the night before. They asked if they should attend class that day in order to take their biology final exam.

16. [mcq, 1 pt] Which of the following is best practice for investigating a potential food-borne illness such as the one in this case?
    A) Take a food history that includes only the last food or meal consumed before the onset of illness.
    B) Take a 24-hour food history that includes all the foods, beverages, meals consumed 24 hours before the onset of illness.
    C) Take a 5-day food history because the incubation period for diarrheal diseases tend to be longer.
    D) No food history needs to be collected.

17. [mcq, 1 pt] Upon investigation, the health department worker learns some additional information. Which of the following pieces of information would most likely lead to the health department worker concluding no further investigation is warranted?
    A) Seven additional complaints have been made the day before by students with similar symptoms who also attend the same university
    B) The students’ symptoms become much more severe and they need to report to the emergency department
    C) The roommate indicates she has a sore throat, fever, and chills along with her continued nausea and vomiting, while the caller describes having had diarrhea overnight but no other signs or symptoms.
    D) A foodhandler at the dining hall where the caller and her roommate frequently eat has been diagnosed with a confirmed case of Norovirus.
