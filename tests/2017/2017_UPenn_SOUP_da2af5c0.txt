[STIMULUS] **CASE STUDY 1: Wall Street Syndrome outbreak at Princeton University**

Wall Street Syndrome (WSS), a disease of unknown cause, has emerged as an increasingly prevalent condition at several Ivy League institutions. Weaker indications for WSS may include overscheduling, academic distress, daily suit-wearing, and involvement in business fraternities. Students who probably have WSS may have a strong desire to transfer to economics or finance-related majors; Financial Engineering, in particular, is a major at Princeton University that is strongly associated with WSS. Applications to finance internships and interviewing for them are also probable indicators. Confirmed cases must have at least an internship at a top financial or consulting firm, a GPA above 3.7, and a completed LinkedIn profile. Of course, having a job offer from a Wall Street firm also confirms WSS.

The students and faculty at PU alerted the administration in 2001 that they had been observing significantly elevated incidence of Wall Street Syndrome cases.

P2-1. [frq, 5 pt] **Construct** a working case definition for WSS based on the information presented.

[STIMULUS] PU administrators, in conjunction with the New Jersey Department of Public Health (NJDPH), established surveillance on WSS in which the University Health Service was supposed to report all cases to the NJDPH. This surveillance continued for five years.

[STIMULUS] **CASE STUDY 2: Acute gastrointestinal illness at Penn**

On the 19th of October 2016, the president of Mongolia Tsakhiagiin Elbegdorj visited the University of Pennsylvania to give a keynote lecture and meet with administrators and key members of the Trustees of the university. A magnificent banquet of the best cuisine Bon Appetit (Penn's dining provider) could offer was served, and received rave reviews from all who sampled their fare.

Two days later, Student Health Service (SHS) sent a notice that a high number of students had reported symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.
